Each year, Kaspersky publishes an analysis of cyber threats specifically targeting the African continent. The 2026 edition confirms a trend that IT security professionals have been observing for several years: attacks are no longer simply exploiting easy targets. They are adapting, specializing, and now reaching sectors that were previously little exposed.

## Banking trojans leading detection counts

Sub-Saharan and West Africa account for the highest number of mobile banking malware detections. Families such as Anubis, SpyNote and their variants continue to circulate through counterfeit applications distributed outside official stores, frequently imitating popular mobile payment apps in the region (MTN Mobile Money, Orange Money, Wave).

The objective is straightforward: intercept login credentials, bypass SMS-based two-factor authentication, and drain accounts before the user realizes what has happened. In markets where mobile money has become the primary financial infrastructure for millions of people, the potential impact is significant.

## Ransomware rising against SMEs

Large enterprises and public institutions have historically been the primary targets for ransomware groups. This logic is shifting. African SMEs, less well protected and often with limited resources to respond to an attack, have become attractive targets for groups seeking smaller but easier ransoms.

Kaspersky identifies several recurring entry vectors: exposed RDP services with weak passwords, unpatched software on Windows systems, and targeted phishing campaigns impersonating local tax authorities or known suppliers.

## BEC attacks advancing in formal businesses

Business email compromise (BEC) attacks are no longer limited to multinationals. Kaspersky teams document a notable increase among mid-sized companies across Anglophone and Francophone Africa. These attacks exploit the implicit trust in email exchanges to redirect payments, modify bank details in supplier systems, or impersonate executives.

The sophistication of these attacks has increased: emails are now written in local languages, in a professional style tailored to the target, without the obvious mistakes that once made them easy to identify.

## What Kaspersky recommends

The report's recommendations do not change fundamentally, but their priority order is evolving:

- **Mobile endpoint protection** is becoming as critical as workstation security. Personal devices used for professional purposes (BYOD) are an underestimated entry point.
- **Ongoing training** for non-IT staff remains the least expensive and most effective lever against phishing and BEC.
- **Network segmentation** limits damage when a workstation or server is compromised.
- **Offline backups** remain the only reliable response to ransomware that has successfully executed.

## Reading this report in the African context

What makes this report particularly useful for IT decision-makers in Africa is its geographic granularity. Kaspersky distinguishes threat profiles by region: North Africa faces greater exposure to state-linked attacks and sophisticated malware, while West and Central Africa concentrate mobile banking trojan detections.

Understanding which threat profile your organization faces based on its sector and geography is the prerequisite for any coherent security strategy.
