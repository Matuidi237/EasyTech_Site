In October 2026, Interpol announced the results of the third edition of Operation Serengeti: an additional $105 million recovered, criminal networks dismantled across the continent. Since the program launched in 2024, total reported recoveries have exceeded $246 million. Behind these figures lies a structural lesson for every IT leader in Africa.

## Three operations, a cumulative record

Operation Serengeti I, conducted in September and October 2024, mobilized Interpol, Afripol and authorities across 19 African countries. It resulted in 1,006 arrests, the dismantlement of 134,089 malicious infrastructures and networks, and the identification of more than 35,000 victims. Targeted threats included ransomware, business email compromise (BEC), digital extortion and online scams.

The 2.0 edition, conducted from June through August 2025, extended the operation to 18 African countries and the United Kingdom. It produced 1,209 additional arrests, 11,432 neutralized infrastructures, $97.4 million recovered and nearly 88,000 new victims identified.

Serengeti III confirms the trajectory: each operation builds on the intelligence gathered during previous ones to target criminal groups, their infrastructure and financial flows with greater precision.

## The central role of FortiGuard Labs

Fortinet has collaborated with Interpol since 2015 and became an official INTERPOL Gateway partner in 2018. For each Serengeti operation, FortiGuard Labs teams provided concrete technical intelligence: indicators of compromise, command-and-control infrastructure details, and forensic analysis enabling investigators to link malicious activity to identified actors.

Beyond raw data, Fortinet organized hands-on workshops for law enforcement officers covering open-source intelligence (OSINT), cryptocurrency investigations and ransomware analysis. This capacity building is essential: technical intelligence only has value when investigators know how to apply it in judicial proceedings.

## The Cybercrime Atlas: mapping to disrupt

Fortinet is a founding grantor of the Cybercrime Atlas, a World Economic Forum initiative. This platform brings together experts from multiple sectors to share open-source research and build a map of cybercriminal operations: infrastructure, relationships between groups, attack patterns.

The objective is strategic. Understanding how a criminal group is organized, how it is funded and where its vulnerabilities lie allows authorities to act in a coordinated way across borders, rather than responding to incidents in isolation.

## What this means for African businesses

These operations target organized networks, but the victims are mostly businesses, financial institutions and individuals. The BEC scams, ransomware and online fraud identified during Serengeti operations are exactly the threats that IT managers face daily from Nairobi to Douala, from Lagos to Abidjan.

The practical conclusion is twofold. First, international collaboration works and is beginning to produce measurable results across the continent. Second, organizations cannot wait for law enforcement to solve the problem for them. A strong defensive posture, up-to-date detection tools and trained teams remain the first line of defense.
