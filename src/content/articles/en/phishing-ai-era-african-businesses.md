For years, phishing awareness relied on a simple principle: spot the spelling errors, the awkward tone, the suspicious sender. That approach is no longer sufficient. AI-generated messages are fluent, personalized, and modeled on the normal language of your organization.

The real question is no longer "does this message look professional?" but "is this request legitimate, and have I verified who is making it?"

## The message is only the entry point

Phishing remains effective because it opens the door to multiple forms of compromise: credential theft, malware infection, payment fraud, account takeover. AI has not changed those objectives. It has improved the quality, personalization, and scale of the lure.

FortiGuard Labs identified more than 13,000 FIFA World Cup 2026-related domains registered between January and May, with 8.8% classified as malicious or suspicious: fake tickets, fraudulent job ads, booby-trapped streaming links. Credible campaigns, built quickly with AI.

For African organizations, where wire transfers and supplier exchanges over WhatsApp are common, the risk is especially high.

## AI makes targeted spear phishing scalable

Targeted spear phishing once required significant research effort. AI reduces that effort to a few minutes: parsing LinkedIn profiles, mapping org charts, generating a message tailored to the target's context.

Voice and video impersonation technologies are now widely accessible. An audio call mimicking your CFO's voice requesting an urgent transfer is no longer science fiction.

## Verify the request, not just the message

Faced with any communication requesting credentials, a transfer, sensitive data, an authentication approval, or urgent action, apply these reflexes:

- **Slow down.** Urgency is a classic manipulation technique.
- **Verify independently.** Do not use the phone number or link from the suspicious message. Contact the person through a known, trusted channel.
- **Question the context.** Is this request unusual? Does the destination domain match the organization it claims to represent?

## Train for decisions, not just recognition

Phishing simulations remain the most effective tool for measuring how employees respond to realistic scenarios. They identify risky behaviors and allow training to be tailored accordingly.

FortiSAT (Fortinet Security Awareness and Training) combines phishing simulations, individual risk scores, and targeted remediation. Those scores can feed into other Fortinet solutions to automatically tighten controls for high-risk users.

The goal is straightforward: Pause. Verify. Report. Any unexpected request involving credentials, money, sensitive data, or urgent action deserves independent verification before it is acted upon.
