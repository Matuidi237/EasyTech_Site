Employees complete mandatory training, learn to identify common threats, and receive reminders about best practices. This basic approach remains necessary, but expectations are evolving rapidly.

It is no longer enough to know whether employees completed their training. Organizations now ask whether training actually changes behavior, reduces incidents, and strengthens resilience against cyber threats.

## Why human behavior remains decisive

Employees decide whether to click a link, report a message, verify a request, share data, or follow a security process. In cloud, collaborative, remote work, and AI-driven environments, these decisions occur constantly, often under time pressure.

According to Fortinet's 2025 Global Security Awareness and Training Research Report, **95% of decision-makers believe better awareness would help reduce cyberattacks**. The same proportion of executive leaders supports these programs. Security awareness is no longer seen as a compliance obligation: it is a strategic investment in reducing human risk.

## Measuring what actually matters

Completion rates remain important but represent only part of the picture. Completing a module does not guarantee that an employee will recognize a convincing phishing message or avoid sharing sensitive data through an unapproved channel.

The 2025 report shows organizations are now measuring outcomes more directly:

- **67%** report a moderate or significant decrease in incidents since starting security awareness programs
- **53%** cite a reduction in security incidents as the primary measure of effectiveness
- **52%** track employee feedback, **50%** track security audits

Yet only **6%** of organizations achieve 100% training completion, and **69%** of leaders still feel employees lack sufficient awareness. The gap between knowledge and behavior remains the real challenge.

## The threat evolves, training must follow

AI is accelerating both the sophistication of attacks and the complexity of employees' daily decisions. Organizations must prepare their teams to recognize AI-enhanced phishing, impersonation, and social engineering, while also helping them use AI tools responsibly.

The report shows **94% of respondents hold regular security awareness sessions**: 46% quarterly and 32% monthly. Short sessions, simulations, and targeted reinforcement keep security front of mind without adding excessive burden to employees' schedules.

## From awareness to operational resilience

A resilient workforce can recognize threats, protect sensitive data, use AI responsibly, report suspicious activity, and adapt to technological change.
